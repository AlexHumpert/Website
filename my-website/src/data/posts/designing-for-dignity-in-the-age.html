The Expanding Canvas of Marketing

“The canvas just got much bigger. And the number of tools we must paint with has drastically increased.”

At VMLMAP, a marketing automation and personalization center of excellence based in Copenhagen, we support Fortune 500 companies in humanizing the relationship between their brands and their customers.

On a day-to-day basis, this involves designing processes that bring together people, tools, and data to deliver personalized experiences, and crafting the brand moments that make existing and future customers fall in love and remain loyal.

The advent of GenAI (generative artificial intelligence), and more recently, Agentic systems powered by GenAI, represents a paradigm shift in how we think about and design the co-existence of humans and machines - both in the internal processes that deliver experiences and in the lived experiences customers have with brands every day.

In other words: the canvas just got much bigger. And the number of tools we must paint with has drastically increased.

From Building Fast to Thinking Deep

The Agentic AI Lab at VMLMAP was established precisely to help clients navigate this rapidly evolving terrain, bringing together designers and engineers to explore what’s possible and make it real. From redesigning workflows around net-new Agentic AI capabilities to developing GenAI-powered consumer journeys fueled by first- and third-party data, our mission is to chart new ground.

For the past two years, the excitement of this new tech launched me into tinkering mode: building what I could imagine, exploring the art of the possible at the speed of thought - now quite literally achievable with the help of coding agents.

“Exploring the art of the possible at the speed of thought - now quite literally achievable with coding agents.”

But coming into the summer break, I realized I had been neglecting more fundamental questions. The pause brought space for reflection, and with it, questions like:

  • Who are we designing for?

  • What principles are guiding our design?

  • How do we blend the best of what humans and machines have to offer?

I went into think thinking only about optimizing against business metrics, but I hadn’t really stopped to ask if I could do more.

Beyond Cynicism: What Are We Really Designing?

The obvious (and somewhat cynical) answer is that we’re designing solutions to improve profitability by driving revenue through more efficient marketing. In other words: selling behavior modification at a low cost.

But I challenged myself to look beyond that cynicism, to find something redeeming in the work we deliver.

Yes, this is marketing. We’re not saving the world. But that doesn’t mean we shouldn’t hold ourselves to higher values, and embed them at a foundational level in both the processes we design and the consumer experiences we create.

Revisiting the Thinkers Who Shaped Our Tools

“Technology, when aligned with Enlightenment values, can move us beyond process optimization - and toward preserving human autonomy.”

To help me think through these more fundamental questions, I revisited the classical Enlightenment thinkers I studied during my undergraduate years: Immanuel Kant, John Locke, Mary Wollstonecraft, as well as the pioneers of the computer revolution in the 20th century: Vannevar Bush, Norbert Wiener, and Joseph Licklider.

I was struck by how much the ideas of Bush, Wiener, and Licklider, especially their visions of computers as tools to amplify human capabilities, echo the Enlightenment’s emphasis on individual freedom and autonomy.

Bush and the Sovereignty of Thought

In As We May Think (1945), Bush’s concept of the Memex envisioned individuals creating their own associative “trails of thought.” This closely resembles Locke’s emphasis on the sovereignty of the mind and the right to pursue knowledge free from imposed hierarchies. By enabling individuals to extend memory and reasoning, Bush upheld the Enlightenment belief in rational self-determination.

Wiener and the Ethics of Automation

In The Human Use of Human Beings (1950), Wiener warned against reducing humans to cogs in automated systems. He argued that technology must serve human dignity rather than erode it, echoing Kant’s imperative that people must always be treated as ends in themselves. His stance reminds us of the intrinsic rights of humans in the face of powerful new machines.

“Wiener’s ethical stance reminds us: humans must never be treated merely as means—even in automated systems.”

Licklider and Human Flourishing

In Man-Computer Symbiosis (1960), Licklider envisioned a future where computers handled repetitive tasks, freeing humans to focus on creative and intellectual pursuits. This aligns with Wollstonecraft’s call for emancipation through education and the cultivation of reason, suggesting that autonomy flourishes when individuals are empowered to think, imagine, and create.

“Autonomy flourishes when individuals are empowered to think, imagine, and create beyond mere subsistence.”

Human Autonomy in Marketing? Absolutely.

Reading these works reminded me that technology, when aligned with Enlightenment values, can move us beyond optimization for efficiency, and toward preserving and extending human autonomy.

So how is human dignity and autonomy relevant to the design of marketing systems?

In a few words: you and me.

“We must remember: we’re designing for ourselves.”

Humans are at the center of the systems we build. But our humanity is often obscured, reduced to “employees” or “consumers,” or discussed solely in terms of cost and revenue. We must remember: we’re designing for ourselves!

Call me crazy, but I have a nagging feeling that ignoring human dignity in how we design work and brand interactions will lead to worse outcomes, both for people and for business.

A Question Worth Pursuing

So, the question is:

Given that new technologies have expanded the design space for human–computer interaction, how can we use Agentic AI to embed principles of dignity and autonomy into marketing processes and consumer experiences?

This isn’t a trivial question. And I don’t claim to have solid answers.

But it does feel like the beginning of an exciting research direction, one that the Agentic AI Lab can meaningfully contribute to.

To help me tackle this big topic, I challenged myself to formulate this as a research proposal for the Lab. Whether or not we explicitly go after these questions, I can at least make it my side-quest to ensure they inform our practice.

Five-Year Research Proposal: Designing Human-Centered Agentic AI in Marketing

As artificial intelligence reshapes how work gets done, we stand at a critical juncture. The design choices we make now will determine whether AI amplifies human potential - or diminishes it.

This research program explores the frontier of human-AI collaboration within two domains:

  1. Marketing automation systems: shaping how employees experience work

  2. Marketing personalization systems: shaping how consumers experience brands

A special emphasis is placed on designing Agentic AI systems that enhance human strengths, eliminate drudgery, and reduce intrusion in brand interactions.

Pillar 1: Redesigning Marketing Workflows for Human–AI Collaboration

Core Problem

Humans and machines excel at different tasks. How can we design workflows that let each do what they do best?

Research Questions

  1. What tasks should remain human vs. AI-automated?

  2. How do we balance human autonomy with brand guardrails?

  3. How can we keep humans in the loop without bottlenecks?

  4. What levels of AI autonomy protect brand integrity and trust?

  5. How should AI-human collaboration vary across roles?

  6. What new skills must marketers develop to thrive?

Practical Applications

  • Workflow audit frameworks

  • Human–AI collaboration design principles

  • Training programs for AI-augmented teams

Pillar 2: Designing Personalization That Respects Attention

Core Problem

As AI interfaces (e.g. Perplexity, ChatGPT) become new touchpoints, how can brands meet needs without violating attention or autonomy?

Research Questions

  1. How will AI assistants reshape brand-consumer relationships?

  2. What new personalization is possible through AI mediation?

  3. How can brands remain relevant when intermediated by AIs?

  4. What ethical principles should guide AI-driven personalization?

  5. How should media budgets evolve?

  6. What new metrics will define success?

Practical Applications

  • Brand presence strategies for AI-mediated interfaces

  • Ethical personalization frameworks

  • Updated consumer journey maps

Human-Centered Design at the Core

Both pillars of this research are grounded in human-centered design, treating employees and consumers as full people, not abstractions.

Inspired by Denmark’s thoughtful design heritage, we aim to position VMLMAP as a leader in designing dignified human–digital interactions.

Value to VMLMAP and the Industry

This research program strengthens MAP’s positioning at the forefront of AI transformation - while reaffirming our humanist roots.

It enables us to:

  • Help clients adopt Agentic AI without losing creative control

  • Differentiate through ethical AI integration

  • Launch new services in AI-human workflow design

  • Future-proof our teams in an AI-driven landscape

Our Place in a Larger Tradition

This work stands on the shoulders of thinkers like Vannevar Bush, Norbert Wiener, and Joseph Licklider, and draws from Enlightenment voices who championed creativity, reason, and free inquiry in the face of institutional and technological power.

By aligning VMLMAP with this humanistic tradition, we become more than just marketers or technologists, we become designers of systems that honor human potential.
