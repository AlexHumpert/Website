Sholto Douglas, Research / Software Engineer at Google DeepMind made a striking point on the Dwarkesh Podcast in response to the question - “If we're living in this world (increasingly sophisticated AI models surpassing human capabilities across domains and solving long-horizon tasks), what is your advice to somebody early in their career, or a student in college? What should they be planning on doing?” (2:23:30).

“It's worth considering the spectrum of possible worlds and preparing yourself for that. The action that I think is the highest EV (expected value) in that case is that at a minimum you're about to get dramatically more leverage… Already the startups in YC (Y-Combinator) are writing huge amounts of their code with Claude.

What challenges, what causes do you want to change in the world with that added leverage? If you had 10 engineers at your beck, and call, what would you do? If you had a company at your beck and call, what would that enable you to do? What problems, and domains suddenly become tractable? That's the world you want to prepare for… Think hard about what challenges you want to solve in this world.”

For me, it's not climate change, poverty, or hunger. It's the ability for human collaboration at scale.

The Double-Edged Sword of Connection

Our species is experiencing the weaving of new communication systems into its social fabric, flooding it with untruths at impressively high fidelity. We hoped these systems would bring our larger human family together. Instead, they seem to be tearing our social fabric apart.

I'm referring to the ascendance of alternative media over the last twenty years: podcasts, social media, streaming platforms. These new systems inspire both optimism and pessimism for the hope of a kind and collaborative human species.

Over the course of a few decades, almost every member of our species has been afforded the capability to broadcast to every other member. One could not wish for a greater enabler of human connection—a digital highway connecting all members of the human family, with trillions of bits flying through the air, each carrying our shared fundamental desire to leave a better world behind for our children than we found, despite the varied forms this vision may take.

There was a slim hope that even though our different views of a "better world" may diverge dramatically, the universal commonalities reflected in these digital exchanges would rise above our differences. They would serve as the common ground needed to allow members of different communities to incrementally compromise their way toward a peaceful mode of living—one that respects each human's positive right to be unique and grants them the inalienable rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.

The Charge of Naivety

At present, it's fashionable to charge anyone who still holds such optimism as "naive" or “impractical.”

On one hand, I agree. An end-state where we all hold hands and walk off into the sunset is fantasy. In fact, if we ever reach this end-state, it will likely be achieved by the complete eradication of difference between members of our species. The eradication of compromise. I don't want to live in that world—a cold, static, uninteresting place with culture and art mandated from the top down. Art decided by committee ceases to be art. It becomes repression.

On the other hand, charges of naivety reveal the misunderstanding of what a vision is and how it functions to serve a cause. The purpose of a vision is to make an abstract hope accessible and turn it into a rallying cry that justifies the unimaginable effort required to pursue it. A vision guides the innumerable difficult decisions and sacrifices that must be made along the way.

The Paradox of Vision

Here lies the paradox: the earnest pursuit of a vision requires absolute belief that it is achievable. Otherwise, one would not put in the effort required to pursue it. But it also requires the wisdom to realize that the gift awarded to those who pursue it—and to the wider society—is not in the attainment of the vision, but in its pursuit.

The Path Forward

So I choose this intractable problem of human collaboration at scale, not because I believe we will solve it completely, but because the attempt itself is what matters.

In fact, the global nature of our most most stubborn and existential challenges - hunger, global warming, poverty, disease - demand that all members of the human species collaborate.

The digital tools that have divided us also offer unprecedented opportunity. With AI as our ally, we can now tackle problems that seemed insurmountable to previous generations. We can experiment with new forms of dialogue, new mechanisms for finding common ground, new ways of preserving what makes us unique while celebrating what makes us one.

The question is not whether we will create a perfect world, but whether we will choose to keep building a better one. In that choice—in that daily commitment to the impossible dream of human collaboration—lies both our challenge and our hope.

So, what is that seemingly intractable problem you want to go after?
